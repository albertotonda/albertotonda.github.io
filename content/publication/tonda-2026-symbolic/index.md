title: Symbolic Regression Can Perform on Par with Tree-based Ensembles in Regression
  Problems
authors:
- Alberto Tonda
- Evelyne Lutton
date: '2026-01-01'
publishDate: '2026-08-14T08:20:29.952465Z'
publication_types:
- paper-conference
publication: '*Artificial Evolution: 17th International Conference, Évolution Artificielle,
  EA 2026, Nice, France, October 27–29, 2026*'
abstract: 'In machine learning, the state of the art for regression problems is currently
  represented by black-box ensembles of decision trees, such as Random Forest and
  eXtreme Gradient Boosting. Symbolic Regression, an Evolutionary Computation technique,
  can tackle regression problems and deliver human-readable models in the form of
  equations. However, the consensus in the field is that Symbolic Regression is generally
  less effective than tree-based approaches. Providing a fair comparison of these
  techniques is not straightforward, as they employ extremely different paradigms.
  In this work, we propose an empirical evaluation of tree-based ensembles and Symbolic
  Regression, employing state-of-the-art software libraries compared on a curated
  regression benchmark suite, using rigorous statistical tests to assess differences
  in performance. The results reveal that Symbolic Regression with commonly used default
  hyperparameters is indeed generally less performant than tree-based ensembles. Nevertheless,
  we show that when the final model selection method is changed and more computational
  resources are allocated to all techniques, Symbolic Regression becomes more competitive,
  often performing on par with the state of the art. Finally, we verify the interpretability
  of the models discovered by Symbolic Regression for selected problems: An expert
  analysis of the equations discovered during the 10-fold cross-validation uncovers
  common structural elements, eventually delivering a global form.'
